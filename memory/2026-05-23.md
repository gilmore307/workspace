## Pre-compaction flush - Layer 4 event-failure-risk boundary

- After the canonical horizon work, Chentong asked to continue with Layer 4.
- OpenClaw framed Layer 4 as `EventFailureRiskModel`: a narrow risk-conditioning layer that uses already reviewed event-family knowledge to identify when event conditions increase strategy failure risk.
- Durable Layer 4 boundary:
  - It is not a news direction/alpha model.
  - It does not directly predict stock price direction.
  - It does not emit buy/sell/hold, action plans, position sizes, orders, or broker/account mutations.
  - It should not freely discover alpha from arbitrary news during model training.
  - It only uses event families that have passed event-strategy promotion/review and entered the accepted event watch/attention surface.
- Layer 4 inputs should include Layer 1 market regime state, Layer 2 sector/context state, Layer 3 target path/tradability/state-transition state, accepted event-family rules/watchlists, point-in-time visible event metadata/evidence, standardized event interpretations, and applicable strategy/target/sector/market conditions.
- Layer 4 should use the canonical horizons `10min`, `1h`, `1D`, and `1W`.
- Preferred training route:
  - Replay or historical analysis identifies repeated strategy failures under specific event conditions.
  - Layer 10/post-trade analysis proposes event-family failure candidates.
  - `event-strategy-promotion-review` validates evidence before promotion.
  - Accepted event families become eligible Layer 4 training inputs.
  - Layer 4 labels target strategy-failure/risk-conditioning outcomes rather than raw price direction.
- Candidate label/output concepts include `strategy_failure_risk`, `entry_block_pressure`, `exposure_cap_pressure`, `strategy_disable_pressure`, `path_risk_amplifier`, `evidence_quality`, and `applicability_confidence`.
- Candidate vector states include `no_reviewed_event_failure_risk`, `observe_only`, `alpha_conditioning_required`, `entry_block_recommended`, `exposure_cap_recommended`, `strategy_family_disable_recommended`, and `human_review_required`.
- Layer 4 consumes only previously accepted event-risk knowledge; Layer 10 discovers/reviews new event-family failure evidence after fold completion. New event-family rules must only affect later folds and must not leak backward into the current fold.

## Event Source Registry Direction for Future Known Dates and Regimes

- Chentong clarified that the immediate open question was not the abstract treatment of future events, but the practical information sources for knowing future key dates and persistent risk periods.
- Accepted event-source hierarchy:
  - Exchange calendars and hours should come from official NYSE/Nasdaq sources for holidays, closures, early closes, and session timing.
  - Options expiration and triple-witching windows should come from Cboe calendars/rules and be rule-computable where possible.
  - Macro scheduled events should come from official agency calendars such as Federal Reserve/FOMC, BLS, BEA, Census, Treasury auction schedules, and EIA where applicable.
  - Index reconstitution/rebalance sources should come from index-provider calendars and announcements such as FTSE Russell/LSEG, Nasdaq indexes, MSCI, and S&P DJI.

  - Persistent regimes such as tariff wars, sanctions, pandemics, wars/geopolitical escalations, banking stress, and policy crisis windows require official sources plus high-quality news as discovery/update evidence. Examples include USTR/Federal Register, OFAC actions, CDC/WHO, and major news wires/outlets for regime updates.
- Proposed durable artifact: an `event_source_registry` where each event family records `source_priority`, official URL(s), fallback sources, update frequency, `available_time` rule, certainty level, lifecycle type, and `promotion_allowed_by_default = false`.
- Core rule: official calendars know future scheduled dates; news discovers/updates persistent regimes; Layer 10 decides whether the observed event/regime/date has incremental trading-failure explanatory value before Layer 4 may train on it.
